# Strategy & Brand — Wildrye Provisions

> **Worked example.** Wildrye is fictional: a DTC pantry brand selling
> regeneratively-farmed grains, flours and beans, ~$14M revenue, 38% on
> subscription. Read this to see how specific a node file has to be before an
> agent can actually work from it. The blank version is [`/strategy.md`](../../strategy.md).

**Last reviewed:** 2026-09-04 by J. Cook (CMO) · **Next review:** 2026-12-01

## Positioning and the promise

**For** home cooks who already buy organic and have started reading past the
label
**who** suspect "organic" stopped meaning much and can't tell which brands
actually farm differently,
**we are** the pantry brand that names the farm and the practice on every bag
**that** lets you trace what you're eating back to the field it grew in.
**Unlike** Bob's Red Mill or a co-op bulk bin,
**we** publish soil carbon and yield data per farm, per season, and we pay the
farmer above commodity price to get it.

**The promise in one sentence:**
You will know exactly whose ground your food came from, and that ground is
measurably better than it was five years ago.

**Proof we can actually keep it:**

1. **Named farms.** Every SKU carries a farm name, county, and harvest date.
   Twelve farms, all under contract, all visited annually by a human on staff.
2. **Published soil data.** Organic matter and infiltration rates per field,
   updated each season at wildrye.com/ground. Third-party sampled, not
   self-reported.
3. **Above-commodity contracts.** We pay a 22% premium on a three-year
   guarantee. The contracts are public. This is the number competitors can't
   copy without changing their cost structure.

**What this positioning costs us:** we cannot expand SKUs faster than we can
contract farms. Merchandising will push on this every quarter. The answer is no.

## Voice and tone — human owned

> Do not delegate this section. An agent applies it; a human decides it.

**We sound like:** a farmer explaining something to a neighbor who asked a good
question. Plain, specific, unhurried. We use numbers instead of adjectives. We
assume the reader is smart and busy.

**We never sound like:** a wellness brand. No "nourish," no "journey," no
"clean," no em-dash-and-ellipsis breathlessness. We do not perform reverence for
food. We do not use the word "artisanal" under any circumstance.

| We say | We don't say |
| --- | --- |
| Grown by the Hollands, Chouteau County | Small-batch, family-farmed goodness |
| 4.1% organic matter, up from 2.8% in 2021 | Regenerative, nutrient-dense soil |
| It's $9 a bag because we pay $2.40 over board | Premium quality you can taste |
| We were out of stock for six weeks. Here's why. | Thanks for your patience! |
| Rye flour behaves differently. Here's how to adjust. | Elevate your baking |

**Tone by situation:**

- **Launch** — matter-of-fact. Lead with what changed, not that it's exciting.
- **Bad news** (stockout, price increase, crop failure) — say it first, in the
  subject line, with the reason and the number. Never bury it. Never apologize
  twice. A crop failure is not a customer service failure; explain the weather.
- **Support** — answer the question in the first sentence, then context.
- **Sales** — we don't have a sales voice. If a piece reads like it's selling,
  it's off-brand. State what the thing is and what it costs.

## Look and feel, visual standards

- **Primary color:** Field ochre `#B7852F`. Ink `#1C1B18` for text. One accent,
  never two.
- **Type:** Untitled Serif for headlines, Söhne for body. Numbers are always
  tabular — data is the brand, so it has to line up.
- **Photography vs. illustration:** Photography only, and only of real farms,
  real fields, real hands, shot on our own trips. No stock. No illustration of
  wheat. If we don't have a real photo of it, we run type on ochre instead.
- **What a wrong-looking asset looks like:** kraft paper texture, hand-lettered
  script, a mason jar, sunlight flare through a wheat field, a smiling model
  holding a bowl. Any of these means the agent reached for the category default
  instead of our library. Reject and regenerate.

## Competitive analysis and where you win

> Agent-maintained. Refreshed monthly; a human reads it before it changes
> anything downstream. Last refresh: 2026-09-01.

| Competitor | Their claim | Where they're strong | Where we win |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bob's Red Mill | Employee-owned, huge range | Distribution, price, trust, 400+ SKUs | They can't name a farm. Scale is the reason and it's structural |
| Anson Mills | Heirloom varieties, chef credibility | Genuine provenance, chef relationships | They sell to restaurants first; DTC experience is an afterthought |
| Local co-op bulk | Cheapest per pound, zero packaging | Price, community, no shipping | No traceability at all past "organic," and no data |
| Thrive Market | Convenience, membership economics | Basket size, subscription mechanics | Aggregator — they carry brands, they aren't one. No farm relationships |

**The gap we are claiming:** provenance with *evidence*. Everyone in the
category claims a story; nobody publishes a dataset. The moat is the twelve
contracts and the sampling program, not the copy.

**Where we're genuinely weak:** SKU count, shipping cost on heavy goods, and
brand awareness outside the Mountain West. Do not write copy that pretends
otherwise.

## What this brand will not do

> The section agents need most. Written so a draft can be checked against it
> without asking a human.

- **We will not use the word "clean," "pure," "wholesome," or "artisanal."**
  Flag any draft containing them.
- **We will not discount below 10%, ever, and never sitewide.** Our farmer
  contracts are fixed; a sitewide sale comes out of margin, and a brand that
  discounts teaches people to wait. Retention may offer a first-subscription
  credit only — see `/customers/retention.md`.
- **We will not make a health claim.** Not "nutrient-dense," not "better for
  you," not "supports gut health." We sell traceability, not nutrition science.
  Any health claim is a legal escalation, tier three.
- **We will not imply carbon neutrality or offset anything.** We publish soil
  data. We do not translate it into a climate claim we can't defend.
- **We will not name a farm we don't have a current contract with**, including
  in retrospectives about past harvests.
- **We will not run influencer content where the creator hasn't cooked with the
  product.** Unboxing is not proof. See `/campaigns/influencer.md`.
- **We will not blame a supplier publicly.** If a crop failed, the reason is the
  weather or our own planning.
